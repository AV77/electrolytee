01 · The electrical nature of life

The resting membrane potential.

01 · The electrical nature of life

Every cell has an electrical difference.

Resting membrane potential

≈ -70 mV

Typical value for a large myelenated neuron at rest. The inside is negative relative to the outside.

  • Unequal ion distribution
  • Selective membrane permeability
  • Na⁺/K⁺ pump maintains the gradients
  • Every living cell maintains an electrical difference across its cell membrane.
  • This is called the resting membrane potential.
  • In a typical neuron, it is approximately minus 70 millivolts, meaning that the inside is negative relative to the outside.
  • This potential is created by unequal ion distribution and selective membrane permeability.
  • The sodium-potassium pump maintains the concentration gradients that make this possible.
  • Importantly, different cell types have different resting membrane potentials.
  • RMP of large myelinated neuron is -90mV
Clarify that - 70 mV is a typical neuronal value, not a universal value for every cell.
01 · The electrical nature of life

Why is the inside negative?

K⁺ leakage

At rest, the membrane is much more permeable to K⁺ than to Na⁺. K⁺ tends to leave the cell, leaving behind relatively more negative charge.

K⁺ moves out→inside becomes negative

Na⁺-K⁺ pump

More positive charges are pumped outside than inside

01 · The electrical nature of life

Why is the inside negative?

Impermeant Anions (Gibbs-Donnan Phenomenon)

Inside the cell, are many negatively charged ions that cannot pass through the membrane channels.

Examples include:
  • Proteins
  • Organic phosphate compounds
  • Sulphate compounds

01 · The electrical nature of life

The action potential.

A rapid, temporary change in membrane voltage that carries an electrical signal.

Schematic neuron trace; values and shape are approximate.

  • Now let's see what happens when a neuron is stimulated.
  • REST
  • The action potential begins when the membrane reaches its threshold, approximately minus 55 millivolts in a typical neuron.
  • DEPOLARISATION
  • Voltage-gated sodium channels open, allowing sodium to enter the cell.
  • The membrane potential rapidly becomes positive.
  • At the peak, sodium channels become inactivated, while voltage-gated potassium channels are open.
  • REPOLARISATION
  • During repolarization, potassium moves out of the cell, bringing the membrane potential back down.
  • HYPERPOLARISATION
  • Sometimes potassium continues to leave briefly, producing hyperpolarization.
  • Finally, the membrane returns towards its resting potential.
  • This entire sequence is what we call an action potential.
01 · The electrical nature of life

The rules of the game.

01 · The electrical nature of life

Two rules make the signal reliable.

All-or-none law

If threshold is reached at any point on the membrane of a normal fiber, a full action potential occurs over the entire membrane. If threshold is not reached, it does not. A stronger stimulus does not make that action potential taller; it can increase firing frequency.

Refractory period

The nerve can not respond to a second stimulus during much of the action potential, and even longer. This is referred to as the refactory period.

  • The action potential follows the all-or-none law.
  • If the stimulus fails to reach threshold, there is no action potential.
  • Once threshold is reached, a full action potential is generated.
  • Following this, the cell enters a refractory period.
  • During the absolute refractory period, another action potential cannot be generated because sodium channels are inactivated.
  • During the relative refractory period, another action potential is possible, but requires a stronger stimulus.
  • This also helps ensure proper electrical signalling.

Ion Inside cell (ICF) Outside cell (ECF) Driving tendency
Na⁺ ≈ 10-15 ≈ 140-145 Enters when Na⁺ channels open
K⁺ ≈ 140 ≈ 4 Leaves through resting leak channels

Sodium

Major ECF cation; dominant determinant of ECF osmolality and important for action-potential upstroke.

Potassium

Major ICF cation; crucial to resting voltage, excitability and repolarization.

  • Sodium is the major cation of extracellular fluid, while potassium is concentrated inside cells.
  • This unequal distribution stores electrochemical energy across the membrane.
  • Sodium is particularly important for extracellular osmolality and water balance.
  • Potassium is especially influential in determining the resting potential of excitable tissues.
  • Remember that the serum potassium concentration is only a small fraction of total body potassium.

02 · The voltage duo

Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase

Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase

  • Present on the cell membraness
  • Transports 3 Na⁺ outside and 2 K⁺ inside cell, while breaking down ATP in the process.

Electrogenic

One net positive charge moved outward per ATP cycle → a small direct negative contribution inside.

Physiological function

Preserves ionic gradients over time

It's major function is maintaining the concentration gradients needed for repeated electrical activity and normal cell volume. The rapid phases of an action potential are produced by ion channels, not by a rapid reversal of the pump.

03 · The muscle factor

The why of it.

03 · The muscle factor

Role of calcium in the body

In Bones and Teeth

Acts as a mechanical framework and a reserve that can be mobilized by hormones like parathyroid hormone (PTH) when blood levels drop.

In Blood and Extracellular Fluid

Mantains vascular tone, nerve impulse conduction, enzyme cofactor activities, and blood coagulation.

In Muscle Cells

Cross-bridge formation and muscle contraction

In Nerves and Synapses

Regulates neurotransmitter release and flows through voltage-gated calcium channels

03 · The muscle factor

The how of it.

03 · The muscle factor

Role of calcium in muscle contraction

In Muscle Cells:

Calcium is released from the sarcoplasmic reticulum into the sarcoplasm to bind with troponin, enabling actin-myosin cross-bridge formation and muscle contraction.

Ca²⁺ released from SR → binds troponin → tropomyosin shifts → actin-myosin cross-bridges form → muscle contracts

The where of it.

04 · The quiet regulator

Chloride warehouses

Major

85% to 90% of the body's chloride is stored in extra cellular fluid:

  • Blood plasma
  • Interstitial fluid
  • Cerebrospinal fluid
  • Gastric juices

Other

The remaining 10% to 15% is located inside cells:

  • Concentration inside most typical tissue cells is low
  • Red blood cells have higher concentrations of chloride in the ICF
Red blood cells (erythrocytes) hold a higher intracellular concentration than other cells because they actively use chloride during the "chloride shift" to transport carbon dioxide.

06 · When balance breaks

Sodium

Hyponatremia

Reduced plasma tonicity causes water to enter brain cells, potentially producing cerebral edema and seizures.

Hypernatremia

Increased plasma tonicity draws water out of brain cells, causing cellular dehydration and neurological dysfunction.

Syndrome of Inappropriate ADH(SIADH)

Excessive ADH-mediated water retention produces dilutional hyponatremia.

06 · When balance breaks

Potassium

Hypokalemia

Altered membrane excitability and cardiac repolarization can cause muscle weakness, U waves and arrhythmias.

Hyperkalemia

Elevated extracellular K⁺ depolarizes the resting membrane potential, potentially impairing cardiac conduction and causing fatal arrhythmias.

Refractory hypokalemia

Concurrent magnesium deficiency promotes renal potassium loss, making hypokalemia difficult to correct.

06 · When balance breaks

Calcium

Hypocalcemia

Reduced extracellular ionized calcium increases neuronal excitability, producing tetany and carpopedal spasms.

Hypercalcemia

Elevated calcium decreases neuromuscular excitability, causing muscle weakness, constipation and altered mental status.

QT interval abnormalities

Hypocalcemia prolongs ventricular repolarization and the QT interval, whereas hypercalcemia shortens them.

06 · When balance breaks

Chloride

Hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis

Gastric HCl loss during prolonged vomiting causes chloride depletion and promotes renal bicarbonate retention.

Hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis

Increased chloride relative to bicarbonate is associated with normal-anion-gap metabolic acidosis.

Cystic fibrosis

Defective CFTR-mediated chloride transport disrupts epithelial fluid secretion, producing thick mucus and elevated sweat chloride.

06 · When balance breaks

Magnesium

Hypomagnesemia

Magnesium deficiency increases neuromuscular excitability and predisposes to cardiac arrhythmias.

Torsades de pointes

Intravenous magnesium sulfate is used to treat this potentially life-threatening polymorphic ventricular tachycardia.

Eclampsia

Magnesium sulfate prevents and treats eclamptic seizures by reducing neuromuscular and central nervous system excitability.
